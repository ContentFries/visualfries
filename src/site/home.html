Cut list

Keep the third try, drop the pauses. Each cut starts and ends on a phrase of the raw take, and every edit point is measured on the sound: program says how far to move one that clips a word.

Shots

full, punch, strip, window, behind, whip. The head is framed from the matte, and each shot opens a slot for your blocks: a panel, a card, a word behind the head.

Transitions

push, wipe, zoom-through, shape-mask, blinds, card. Give one a sound and it lands on the cut. None of them shifts the program's time or its speech.

Keys

Camera and clip moves between keys, and block props that animate. Brand colours mix in OKLCH, so a gradient from one to the other stays saturated halfway.

Sound

Effects on words, beats or a block's own events. Music that ends on a bar. One mix at −14 LUFS and −1 dBTP, with speech, effects and music as stems on request.

Retakes

Pick another try or a new take and render again. Cuts, shots, captions, sounds and transitions follow their words, and the music snaps to the nearest bar again.

Segment cache

The program renders in segments between hard cuts. Change one clip and only the segments it is on screen in are drawn again. Same project, same frames, same samples.

Hand-off

Finishing in an editor? --export inserts writes every clip as a transparent file with 10-frame handles and a manifest that lines up with the rough cut.

A frame is a Svelte component on a canvas of any size. One frame is a post. Five clips are a carousel. Five hundred frames timed to a voice are a reel. Same blocks, same CLI, same brand. Everything here was written by Claude Opus 5.5 and rendered by VisualFries, from one 17-second take.

Every frame is layers. You own all of them.

A matte cuts the speaker out of the footage. That puts a stage behind your head: type can slam in there, a light can glow around your outline, a delayed grey clone can stand next to you. Captions ride on top, timed to the word. Each layer is a Svelte block you can read, edit and re-render. In a program, the behind shot sets this stage for one word at a time, in the middle of a whole edit.

Four ideas. Zero surprises.

A frame is a function of time. VisualFries sets the time, Chromium draws the DOM, ffmpeg writes the file. That is the whole trick, and it is why renders never drift.

Blocks are Svelte

Any visual is a component with props. Text is real DOM: it wraps, kerns and reflows per size. Your brand's colours and fonts and each platform's safe zones arrive as CSS variables, and one line of use:motion gives an element its entrance.

Time comes from words

A cut ends on "the silences", a whip lands on "open-source", a cue on "bar 5" of the music. A new take re-times everything; a phrase that vanished fails with "Did you mean".

Deterministic frames

No timers, no CSS transitions, randomness from seeded noise. check --determinism renders every segment forward, backward and scrambled and compares the frames before you spend a minute on a render.

One CLI, loud failures

Built to be driven by agents: every error says what to fix. A whole program as one MP4 with its mix, PNG stills, or ProRes 4444 alpha inserts with a manifest.json for your editor.

doctorChecks ffmpeg, Playwright, Chromium and the toolchain.
